# Q868 literature assessment

**Checked 2026-11-27.** High confidence in the model match and direct antecedents. Moderate confidence in the bounded finding that no checked work gives the overlapping-parent construction or the sharp ratio for re-optimized Algorithm 1.

1. **Exact source question.** Ali, Yang and Zilles, *Approximation Algorithms for Preference Aggregation Using CP-Nets*, AAAI 2024, pp. 10433–10441. The local cost is total disagreement on swaps at the target binary attribute. Algorithm 1 and Theorem 13, pp. 10437–10438, minimize over all tables whose parent set is contained in one input parent set. It re-optimizes by pooling disagreements across full contexts that coincide on that set. The conclusion, p. 10439, explicitly asks for a $4/3$ bound or an instance exceeding it. The revised author preprint includes detailed proofs. Theorem 4 concerns best-input selection. Theorems 16–17 give a different family for which best-input selection is asymptotically a factor 2 from optimal while Algorithm 1 is optimal. Proposition 3 already gives an exact efficient algorithm for fixed input count. [Proceedings PDF, Algorithm 1](https://ojs.aaai.org/index.php/AAAI/article/download/28911/29733#page=5) · [Conclusion](https://ojs.aaai.org/index.php/AAAI/article/download/28911/29733#page=7) · [Author preprint](https://arxiv.org/abs/2312.09162)
2. **Exact ballot antecedent.** Endriss and Grandi, *Binary Aggregation by Selection of the Most Representative Voters*, AAAI 2014, pp. 668–674. Theorem 9 gives the strict factor-2 result on profiles with positive optimum. Example 2, p. 672, gives the identity ballot matrix and exactly $(2t-2)/t$ for selecting a submitted ballot. The new construction's singleton votes form its bit complement. Remaining contexts are unanimous. The older construction already supplies the sharp ballot pattern. The averaged best-input upper bound in the present paper is a finite-count specialization of the standard bound. The new step is to make the pattern persist under re-optimization by specifying overlapping parent sets and a unanimous origin in every exceptional context pair. [Theorem 9](https://cdn.aaai.org/ojs/8798/8798-13-12326-1-2-20201228.pdf#page=4) · [Example 2](https://cdn.aaai.org/ojs/8798/8798-13-12326-1-2-20201228.pdf#page=5)
3. **Bounded-indegree follow-up.** Ali, Ogundare, Yang and Zilles, *Approximation Ratio for Preference Aggregation Using Tree CP-Nets*, AAMAS 2025, pp. 78–86. Section 2 uses 1-bounded to mean at most one parent per attribute. Section 3 defines both rules and states Algorithm 1's dominance over best input. Theorem 5.1, pp. 82 and 84, gives at most $4/3$ for best-input selection on 1-bounded profiles. By dominance it also bounds Algorithm 1 on those profiles. For the present profile at $t=4$, every input has three relevant parents and there is no conflict. [Proceedings PDF](https://www.ifaamas.org/Proceedings/aamas2025/pdfs/p78.pdf)
4. **Closest later primary source.** Ali, *Exact and Approximate Preference Aggregation Using Conditional Preference Networks*, University of Regina PhD thesis, October 2025. The thesis calls the same re-optimizing rule Algorithm 3. Section 3.7, pp. 83–84, again asks the unrestricted $4/3$ question. Chapter 4 gives the bounded-indegree work. Section 5.2, pp. 143–144, identifies overlapping and larger parent sets as directions and leaves broader analysis of the second algorithm open. The thesis names the first algorithm as best-input selection. [University item](https://hdl.handle.net/10294/17240) · [Thesis PDF, §3.7](https://uregina.scholaris.ca/server/api/core/bitstreams/f7b980d4-dac7-4b41-b89a-0bd14ab0ffcb/content#page=99)
5. **Earlier formulation.** Ali, Hamilton, Rayner, Yang and Zilles, *Aggregating Preferences Represented by Conditional Preference Networks*, ADT 2021, pp. 3–18, defines the swap objective and output-size issue. The present counterexample concerns the 2024 paper's specific approximation rule. [Proceedings chapter](https://pure.rug.nl/ws/files/202200570/2021_Book_AlgorithmicDecisionTheory.pdf)
